## Summary

The project is at the data-feasibility stage. The exploratory script in `notebooks/01_saos_feasibility.py` checks scoped SAOS queries, pagination, full-text retrieval, and basic record validation. It uses `requests` and stores raw responses, acquisition metadata, and its report under `tmp/saos_feasibility/`.

`notebooks/02_api_search.py` provides a minimal cell-based example for manually searching one page and inspecting one full judgment. It keeps responses in memory and does not extract labels or assess feasibility automatically.

`notebooks/wymagania.md` defines the ten-year research scope, separate award components, court metadata, locality-size classes using official geographic data, broad case-description fields, evidence requirements, and leakage controls. These are requirements, not a claim that the current prototype implements or validates all of them. The expanded run examined 304 sampled judgments from the decade; numeric PLN amounts appeared in 286 and explicit fact/reasoning boundaries in 98. These are exploratory detection counts, not validated target coverage.

The prototype now retrieves court hierarchy and division metadata and matches seats against the GUGiK BDOT10k locality GeoPackage using Morfeusz inflection analysis. A locality class was resolved for 159 of 307 analyzed documents, including additional reviewed examples; ambiguous matches remain missing. The population measurement year is unavailable, so these classes are a current-source experiment, not verified historical demographics.

Local inference in `notebooks/03_system_one_polish.py` uses pinned basal/Bielik weights and six broad description fields from `configs/dataset/basal.yaml`. Model instructions and categories also live there; SAOS filters, dates, query variants and development/sample selection live in `configs/dataset/api.yaml`. The notebook manifest references both independently of the working directory. The default now classifies every paragraph semantically with neighbouring context; headings are not required. Mixed paragraphs are reconsidered as sentences, and native `extract` fills a JSON schema from accepted factual passages. Each per-judgment `record.json` contains short source descriptions or `brak informacji`; surrounding source sentences preserve negations. Award extraction detects numeric PLN mentions and gives basal the complete sentence/list item with the selected occurrence marked, its section, the operative ruling and up to 600 preceding characters. Joint assignments use only two target types, an unallocated principal, exclusion or uncertainty. Independent component aggregation preserves repeated values without summing them, prefers ruling evidence and checks arithmetic consistency; one final decision checks the assembled proposal. Process/court costs, interest and periodic rent are excluded. Candidate values retain evidence, offsets, model probabilities, and separate missing/uncertain/rejected/unprocessed statuses. The configured paragraph threshold is 0.6; low-confidence extracted descriptions are retained for review. Maximum leakage probability is 0.2, and the independent joint-amount-assignment and whole-award confirmation threshold is 0.6. These thresholds do not establish accuracy or approve training labels. `notebooks/04_gemma.py` implements the last-resort Ollama variant; Gemma inference is deferred until local weights are available.

Before simplifying monetary extraction, the native feature-extraction trial produced six source-grounded descriptions for judgment 554194, including the fracture, continuing symptoms, life impact, personal situation, and family assistance. Judgment 553925 retained factual passages but the extractor returned no descriptions; judgment 254930 had no admitted factual passages. Short batches improved the first case over a single 14,000-character input. The final run took 61.2 seconds after model loading, with 410 cached requests and 23 new requests; this is not fresh-inference throughput. The small section/leakage control scored 6/7 for each task. The 28 automated tests check mechanics and boundaries, not general extraction accuracy.

All contents of `data/` are ignored except `data/.gitkeep`, including package markers and nested directories.

The `notebooks/` and `tmp/` directories are fully ignored, with no file exceptions.

The initial snapshot contains 18 inspected judgments. All passed basic structural checks, but the review found mixed award components, unrelated search results, missing factual detail, and inconsistent dates. Data acquisition is feasible; building a reliable regression dataset remains conditional on a precise target definition, reviewed extraction, and leakage prevention. Search counts do not establish the number of usable training examples.

`PROJECT.md` includes evaluation of one fixed open-weight Polish model for feature extraction and standardization. Basal-mini and basal 4.5B ran locally on three judgments: the initial full-text trials took approximately 51 and 137 seconds after loading weights. This small diagnostic trial does not establish extraction accuracy, calibrated confidence, leakage safety, or regression performance. Database ingestion, vectorization, and training are not implemented.

The intended outputs are the principal amounts awarded by the analyzed judgment: compensation for non-material harm and other one-off damages, separately for the modeled claimant. Their sum is derived; litigation/court costs, interest, and periodic rent are excluded. Current scripts propose two award components with source evidence and a joint check; they do not classify every monetary occurrence. The current monetary classifier no longer requires a fixed word order or numbering format. Conflicting values, ambiguous beneficiaries and appellate reconstruction still require review. A live API/documentation check found no structured award-by-category field: decision/summary are textual and null in the three inspected cases; the enrichment MAX_REFERENCED_MONEY is only the largest mentioned PLN amount.

Before introducing sentence-based monetary inputs, a broader preprocessing audit was recorded in tmp/preprocessing_review/report.md. That audit showed the previous extractor was not ready to generate training labels automatically: it confirmed 6,400 PLN appellate process costs as other damages (model score 0.791) and left known 6,300/17,000 PLN compensation awards unallocated. Screening the 304 sampled documents with rules alone found no compensation component and 38 unallocated principals; this is not full-pipeline recall. Three reviewed injury cases yielded 6/6, 5/6 and 6/6 feature descriptions, with source offsets verified, but no measured field accuracy or corpus-wide leakage guarantee.

- Compare a fixed Polish model against the labeled feature sample before selecting it.
